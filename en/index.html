A soft interface between people and information

The Human Information Systems Laboratory at Osaka Metropolitan University, led by Professor Katsuhiro Honda and Associate Professor Seiki Ubukata, studies data analysis and soft computing. This site introduces our research, faculty, learning demos and the careers of our graduate students.

Our research covers knowledge discovery from large databases, such as questionnaire data and internet content, using methods that fuse fuzzy theory with multivariate analysis; data analysis methods for kansei (affective) information that involves vague human value judgments; agent simulations and learning algorithms based on cognitive and kansei models; and the development of information media.

Axelidea, led by alumnus Dr. Nishida, wins the Regional Award at NEDO’s GENIAC-PRIZE

Axelidea Inc., whose CEO is our laboratory alumnus Dr. Nishida (Ph.D. in Engineering), received the Regional Award, a special prize of the GENIAC-PRIZE, a generative AI program run by the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI) and NEDO. The award was shared with Kuriyama Hosei Co., Ltd.

The winning project, “KATA-LYST AI,” develops an AI agent that turns the tacit knowledge of skilled craftspeople into digital assets. Combining multidimensional data from ultra-compact tactile sensors with video analysis by a Japanese foundation model, it is a skill-transfer platform that makes craftsmanship explicit down to the level of physical laws.

The company has also released “AXELIDEA-QUON-14B-Japanese-v01,” a Japanese-specialized LLM trained on problem-solving patterns distilled from patent documents. It ranks first among non-reasoning 14B-class models on the JA Leaderboard (average of seven Japanese evaluation tasks) (press release, in Japanese).

Research

Finding information: a word-of-mouth travel destination navigator

This system applies collaborative filtering to travel destination recommendation. Collaborative filtering estimates how a user would rate items they have not yet rated, based on the similarity of preferences between users, and finds items recommended specifically for that user. The system reproduces “word of mouth” on a computer: hearing about the things that people with tastes similar to yours say they love. We study theories and techniques for estimating unrated values—that is, missing values—from the other observed values in a matrix of ratings that many users have given to various content according to their own preferences.

Data mining that embraces vagueness

From the viewpoint of soft computing—flexible information processing built on fuzzy theory, which blurs boundaries, and rough set theory, which captures information coarsely—we study data mining and knowledge discovery that reflect the vagueness of human judgment. Now that big data is part of everyday life, there is a growing need for clustering techniques that automatically classify huge amounts of data. By taking the vagueness and uncertainty of classification into account, we can achieve clustering that is more flexible, reliable and safe.

Learning algorithms inspired by human thinking

Modern learning algorithms have advanced by refining, through informatics, the ways in which people learn. Adding a cognitive perspective to various learning algorithms makes learning more efficient. The figure uses arrows to show how units modeled on nerve cells roughly capture the learning process as they learn an S-shaped maze. Making good use of such vagueness, rather than avoiding it, enables more efficient learning.
